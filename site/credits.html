Image credits.

This campaign uses downloaded sculpture photographs and original Notorism teaching studies. The photographs are cropped, masked, converted to luminance, posterized into four tones, and processed with an ordered Bayer dither. Geometric compositions are generated by the Notorism site code.

Head of David

Plaster cast after Michelangelo Buonarroti. Collection and photograph: Statens Museum for Kunst, accession KAS2232. The museum reproduction is released under CC0. David is a Renaissance work, not an ancient Greek sculpture.

Museum photograph and license record · SMK collection

Hebe

Bertel Thorvaldsen, 1816. Thorvaldsens Museum, accession A874. Museum photograph, credited to Thorvaldsens Museum / Jakob Faurvig. The selected photograph is released under CC0. Hebe is a neoclassical sculpture of the Greek goddess.

Photograph and license record · Museum collection

Study of a Hand

Auguste Rodin, modeled circa 1885, cast before 1912. The Metropolitan Museum of Art, object 191852 / accession 12.12.8. Gift of Auguste Rodin, 1912. Photograph: The Metropolitan Museum of Art, public domain through its Open Access program.

Museum photograph and collection record · Open Access policy

Hand of the Colossus of Constantine

Roman marble fragment, Musei Capitolini, accession MC 786. Photograph: Jastrow, 2006, released into the public domain by the photographer. The image is cropped, silhouette-masked, posterized, dithered and rotated to direct attention to the installation text. It is a separate photograph from Rodin's open hand.

Photograph and public-domain declaration · Museum collection

Notorism reference studies

Swiss Design, Brutalism, Dithering, Constructivism, Editorial Layout and Art Deco. Original teaching studies from the local Notorism library, distributed under Apache-2.0. Their palette is adapted to the campaign. They are fictional compositions, not client work.

Type and original design

The eye fragment in the release poster is mirrored horizontally to look toward the headline. Its torn-paper opening is made with CSS layers, irregular clipped edges, fibers and inset shadows. No sculpture anatomy is generated or redrawn.
