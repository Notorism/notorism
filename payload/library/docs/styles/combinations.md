# Combining languages

Choose a dominant language, a composition strategy, and at most a few supporting
treatments. Record which layer owns typography, material, organization, and motion.
The number of combined labels is not a quality metric.

| Combination | Division of responsibilities | Suitable use | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Swiss Design + Brutalism + Dithering | Swiss alignment, raw structural surfaces, raster imagery | Architecture publications and experimental portfolios | Noise or excessive weight obscuring text |
| Neo-Brutalism + Bento Grid | Explicit outlined controls, varied content modules | Creative tools and project collections | Every module becoming equally loud |
| Acid Graphics + Chrome + Kinetic Typography | Expressive display type, metallic focal object, short timed title | Music and experimental campaigns | Unreadable labels and motion overload |
| Brutalism + ASCII Art + Terminal UI | Exposed structure, character-based image, functional command vocabulary | Code archives and genuine developer tools | Fake technical content and inaccessible ASCII |
| Art Deco + Editorial Layout | Geometric framing, composed text-image rhythm | Hospitality, culture, and objects | Ornament replacing product information |
| Biophilic Design + Full-Bleed Design | Nature-specific imagery, controlled edge-to-edge composition | Landscape and environmental subjects | Unsupported ecological claims |

Swiss Design and International Typographic Style overlap historically. Soft UI
and Neumorphism overlap in contemporary usage. Synthwave and Outrun share a
visual family. Distinguish purpose and mechanics rather than inventing false
historical independence. Gradients, bento, and motion can support many styles.
